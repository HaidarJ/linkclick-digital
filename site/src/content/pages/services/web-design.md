## How much does a website cost for a small business?

The cost of a small-business website depends on how many pages and page types it needs, who writes and supplies the content, what the site connects to, and how much search groundwork comes with it. Linkclick Digital scopes each website after a strategy call instead of publishing fixed prices, so the budget goes on the pages your enquiries need.

These are the things that move the price most:

- **Pages and templates.** One campaign landing page is a smaller job than a full site with service, location and article templates.
- **Copy and content.** Copy written for people and for search takes time, and so do photography and video. Finished copy and images from your team make the build smaller.
- **Connections.** Booking calendars, CRM-linked forms, WhatsApp buttons and [AI follow-up on every enquiry form](/services/ai-automation/) each add set-up and testing.
- **Search groundwork.** Replacing a site means mapping old pages to new ones, so the traffic and links they earned have somewhere to go.
- **Compliance review.** If your industry has advertising rules, your compliance lead may need to check the copy, which adds a review round.
- **Ongoing work.** Hosting, updates and conversion testing can run on a monthly retainer.

The contact form asks for a budget band. [Book a strategy call](/contact/) and Linkclick Digital will tell you honestly what that budget can build, and what it can't.

## How long does it take to build a website?

How long a Linkclick Digital website project takes, from agreed brief to launch, depends on its scale: a single landing page is quicker than a full site. On any project, the biggest variable is content, not code.

What keeps a project on time:

- **Content ready early.** Copy, photos, reviews and team details arrive before design starts.
- **One decision-maker.** One person on your side approves each stage, so feedback doesn't conflict.
- **Compliance review booked in.** If your industry has advertising rules, your compliance lead's review is planned from the start.
- **Access sorted early.** Domain, hosting, calendar and CRM access are shared before the build, so connections can be tested.

## What makes a good landing page?

A good landing page gives one kind of visitor one clear reason to act and one easy way to do it. It repeats the promise of the ad or search result that brought them, answers the questions that stop people enquiring, shows real proof, and makes the next step simple on a phone: a short form, a booking slot or a WhatsApp message.

Linkclick Digital builds these six things into every landing page:

### Message match

The headline uses the words of the ad, post or search that sent the visitor. If the ad names a service and a place, the page opens with that service and that place, not a general welcome.

### One action

Each page has one main call to action, repeated as the visitor scrolls. WhatsApp sits alongside it for people who would rather message than fill in a form.

### Proof near the decision

Real reviews, credentials and short [human-led UGC videos](/services/ugc-content/) sit next to the call to action, where doubt is highest. If your industry has advertising rules, every review, testimonial and claim on the page is checked against them before it goes live.

### Answers to the questions that stop enquiries

Cost, process, timing and who the service suits are answered on the page, so the visitor doesn't leave to find out.

### Short forms and fast replies

Forms ask only for what is needed to reply, and can feed straight into AI follow-up or your team's inbox. A page that converts still loses the booking if the reply takes a day.

### Tracking

Every form, booking and WhatsApp tap is tracked, so you can see which ads and pages bring enquiries.

## Built for Google and AI assistants

Linkclick Digital builds websites as static HTML: every word, heading and link is in the page the server sends, with structured data that describes the business and pages that load fast. That makes a site easy for Google and Bing to crawl, and easier for AI assistants such as ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews to read, quote and link to.

### Static HTML

Many websites assemble their content with JavaScript in the browser. A December 2024 Vercel study found that none of the major AI crawlers rendered JavaScript, so content that only appears after a script runs was invisible to them ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/blog/the-rise-of-the-ai-crawler)). On a static page, the content is already in the HTML.

### Structured data

Structured data (schema.org markup) labels what a page is about: the business, its services, its location and its questions and answers. Google says it uses the structured data it finds on the web to understand the content of a page ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data)). Linkclick Digital adds markup that matches what is visible on each page, and nothing that isn't.

### Fast loading

Speed is part of the build, not a fix afterwards: compressed images, little script and layouts that don't jump. Google's Core Web Vitals measure real-world loading, interactivity and visual stability, and Google says they align, with other page experience signals, with what its core ranking systems aim to reward ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals)).

### Search first, AI answers second

Search still sends far more visitors than AI assistants. Across 101,000 sites in 2026, organic search sent 42.75% of website traffic and AI chatbots 0.32% ([SE Ranking](https://seranking.com/blog/ai-traffic-research-study/)). So each site is built to rank first and to be cited second, and the [SEO and AI search service](/services/digital-marketing/seo-ai-search/) keeps both working after launch. The guide on [how to get your business recommended by ChatGPT](/insights/get-recommended-by-chatgpt/) covers the rest.

No website can promise a ranking or an AI citation. A well-built one removes the technical reasons not to be chosen.

## Mobile-first by default

Linkclick Digital designs every website for a phone screen first, then adapts it for tablets and desktops. Google uses the mobile version of a site's content for indexing and ranking ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/mobile/mobile-sites-mobile-first-indexing)), and Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp are phone-first apps, so visitors from UGC ads and messages usually arrive on a small screen.

The mobile page wins or loses the enquiry, so it is designed and approved first. In practice, that means:

- buttons large enough for a thumb, with the main call to action within reach
- text that reads without zooming
- short forms that open the right keyboard and support autofill
- booking and WhatsApp that work in one tap
- video that doesn't autoplay with sound or push the layout around as it loads

## How a website project runs

A Linkclick Digital website project runs in seven steps, from the first call to ongoing improvement. Each step ends with something you approve before the next one starts, so you always know what is being built and why.

1. **Strategy call.** We talk through your business, who the site is for and what a good month looks like.
2. **Page plan.** Linkclick Digital maps the pages, the question each one answers and the one action it should drive, and plans redirects from your old site.
3. **Copy and content.** Copy is written for people and for search, with your team supplying facts and photos. If your industry has advertising rules, the copy is checked against them here, and your compliance lead reviews it.
4. **Design.** Layouts are designed for phones first, using your brand or a new one from the [brand identity add-on](/add-ons/).
5. **Build.** The site is built as static HTML with structured data, analytics and enquiry tracking, and connected to your booking calendar, forms and any AI follow-up.
6. **Test and launch.** Forms, links, redirects, speed and tracking are checked on phones and desktops, then the sitemap goes to Google and Bing.
7. **Improve.** After launch, conversion testing can continue on a monthly retainer, alongside the other [Linkclick Digital services](/services/) that send visitors to the site.
