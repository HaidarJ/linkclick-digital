## What UGC costs in the UK in 2026

In 2026, published UK rate guides put a single UGC video from a creator at £50 to £250 for use on your own channels ([Whito, data checked November 2026](https://whito.co.uk/research/influencer-ugc-rates-uk/)) and £100 to £800 depending on the creator's experience ([FluxNote, updated June 2026](https://fluxnote.io/guides/ugc-creator-rates-uk)). Longer paid-ad usage, extra hooks and editing are often priced on top.

We found no independent UK survey of UGC prices, so the figures below come from rate guides published by companies in the creator market. Treat them as indicative, and always check what a quoted price includes.

| Source and date | What it measures | Published figure |
|---|---|---|
| [Whito](https://whito.co.uk/research/influencer-ugc-rates-uk/), data last verified 9 November 2026 | A UGC video for your own channels, compiled from six 2026 rate guides | £50 to £250 per video |
| [Whito's read of 35 UGC and creator platform pricing pages](https://whito.co.uk/research/influencer-ugc-rates-uk/), 12 October 2026 | Entry prices of the four platforms it lists | £59 to £350 per video; 17 of the 35 platforms published no price |
| [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/ugc-creator-rates-uk), updated 24 June 2026 | One 15 to 60 second video, by creator tier | Beginner £100 to £200; established £200 to £400; expert £350 to £800 |
| [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/ugc-creator-rates-uk), same guide | Three hook variations of one video | £200 to £350 from a beginner, up to £600 to £1,000 from an expert |

Know who is behind each number. Whito Ltd is a UK research publisher that discloses it sells related services; it says its rate range is a compilation of other guides, not original research. FluxNote publishes creator rate guides and doesn't describe its method. Whito also notes that the two platforms whose full cost it could calculate charged £295 and £350, above the rate-guide range ([Whito](https://whito.co.uk/research/influencer-ugc-rates-uk/)), so compare full costs rather than headline prices.

## What drives the price

Five things drive the price of a UGC video: the usage rights you buy, the number of hooks and edits, the creator's experience and any rules your industry adds, the turnaround, and who writes and manages the brief. The same video costs more once you license it to run as a paid ad over a longer period.

### Usage rights

Usage rights set where and for how long you can use a video: organic posts, paid ads, or ads run through the creator's own handle (Meta partnership ads and TikTok Spark Ads, sometimes called whitelisting). FluxNote's UK guide says standard paid digital rights for three to six months are typically included in the base fee, with an extension fee of 30 to 50% of the original fee for each further three-month term ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/ugc-creator-rates-uk)). Influee, a UGC marketplace, prices usage rights at 30 to 50% of the base rate and whitelisting at 30% of the base rate per month, in a guide published in April 2025 ([Influee](https://www.influee.co/gb/blog/ugc-price)).

Bundles differ too. In Whito's table, one platform's £130 entry price included a 12-month paid-media licence, while another's £350 covered full paid and organic use ([Whito](https://whito.co.uk/research/influencer-ugc-rates-uk/)). Get the rights in writing: channels, paid or organic, duration, and whether you receive the raw footage.

### Hooks, edits and variations

Paid social rarely runs on one version of an ad. A hook is the opening line or shot, and testing several hooks on the same body is how you learn what makes people stop and watch. FluxNote lists three hook variations at £200 to £350 from a beginner and £600 to £1,000 from an expert ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/ugc-creator-rates-uk)); Influee's US-dollar guide prices each extra hook or call-to-action variation at $50 ([Influee](https://www.influee.co/gb/blog/ugc-price)). Cutting for Reels, TikTok and Shorts, plus captions and subtitles, adds editing time whoever does it.

### Creator experience and industry rules

Creators with a track record in ads charge more: FluxNote's expert tier starts at £350, above the top of its beginner range ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/ugc-creator-rates-uk)).

Some industries also have extra advertising rules, which can add review time and cost: Whito notes that rule-heavy niches cost more again, without giving figures ([Whito](https://whito.co.uk/research/influencer-ugc-rates-uk/)). Check your industry's rules before you brief a creator, so the script and the person on camera meet them from the start.

### Turnaround, raw footage and extras

Influee's guide adds 25 to 50% for rush jobs and 30 to 50% for raw footage ([Influee](https://www.influee.co/gb/blog/ugc-price)). Ask who pays for product shipping, props and reshoots before you agree a price.

### Who writes the script and runs the shoot

Some prices cover filming only. Briefing, scripting, casting, revisions and approvals take time, and someone has to do them: you, a platform's tools or an agency.

## Creator vs agency

Hiring creators directly means paying only the creator, but you do the casting, briefing, scripting, contracts, compliance checks and editing yourself. A UGC platform sits in between. An agency charges more because it does that work and is accountable for the result, which matters most when you need volume, paid-ad testing or scripts that follow your industry's advertising rules.

| Route | What you pay for | What you still do yourself | Suits you when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct with creators | The creator's fee and usage rights | Finding and vetting creators, briefs, scripts, contracts, editing, compliance checks, ad labels | You have someone in-house to run it |
| UGC platform or marketplace | Access to a creator pool, often at set prices; some add a monthly platform fee | Briefs, creator selection, review, editing, testing | You need simple product videos at low cost |
| UGC agency | Strategy, scripts, casting, filming, editing, rights management and testing | Approvals and final sign-off | You need volume, ad testing or scripts checked against your industry's rules |

Whichever route you choose, the advertising rules apply to your brand as well as the creator. In the UK, the ASA's guide to making clear that ads are ads, developed with the CMA and updated to its fourth edition on 23 October 2026, is written for brands and agencies as well as creators ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/influencers-guide.html)), and the CAP Code says marketing communications must be obviously identifiable as such ([rule 2.1](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/02.html)).

Before you hire anyone, ask:

- Which usage rights are included, for how long, and what does an extension cost?
- How many hooks, edits and formats come with each video?
- Who writes the script, and who checks it against ASA and CAP rules in the UK, and any extra rules for your industry?
- Do you receive the raw footage?
- What happens if a video doesn't match the brief?
- How will paid partnerships be disclosed?

## Is UGC worth it?

UGC is worth it when you will use it: as paid ads you test against each other, on the pages where people decide, and wherever seeing a real person builds trust. It is rarely worth it as a one-off batch with no plan to test or reuse it. Judge it on cost per enquiry or booked call, not views.

Ad spending is moving towards social video and creators. UK social media ad spend rose 21% to £11.5bn in 2025, and video made up 59% of it ([IAB UK, March 2026](https://www.iabuk.com/news-article/digital-adspend-2025-uks-digital-ad-market-reaches-ps405bn)). UK creator partnership revenue reached £966m in 2025 and is forecast to grow 26% to £1.217bn in 2026, according to IAB UK research reported by InPublishing ([InPublishing, September 2026](https://inpublishing.co.uk/articles/uk-creator-revenue-to-surpass-1bn-for-the-first-time-in-2026-26924)). That shows brands are buying it; it doesn't prove it will work for you. Measuring does.

1. **Work out the cost per usable ad.** Divide the total cost of a batch (fees, rights and editing) by the number of distinct versions you can run.
2. **Test against your current best ad.** Give the new versions the same budget and audience.
3. **Compare cost per enquiry or booked call**, not clicks or views.
4. **Reuse the winners** on landing pages, in email and on your Google Business Profile, within the rights you bought.

## Human-led vs AI UGC

AI-generated UGC, made with avatars or synthetic actors, is cheaper to produce, but UK audiences distrust it. In a YouGov poll of 2,012 UK adults in July 2026, 78% said they would trust a brand less if it used AI-generated actors ([YouGov, July 2026](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/55180-half-of-britons-have-seen-ai-generated-actors-in-ads-and-they-dont-like-them)).

The same [YouGov poll](https://yougov.com/en-gb/articles/55180-half-of-britons-have-seen-ai-generated-actors-in-ads-and-they-dont-like-them) found 69% of UK adults have seen ads with AI-generated actors, 76% of those who have seen them don't like them, and 94% think companies should disclose when they use them.

The rules add a second risk. CAP says there is no blanket legal requirement in the UK to disclose AI in ads, but that disclosure alone is very unlikely to fix a fundamentally misleading message, such as an AI image of a product's effect that doesn't match real results ([CAP, May 2025](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/disclosure-of-ai-in-advertising-striking-the-balance-between-creativity-and-responsibility.html)). The CMA's guidance defines a fake review as one that claims to be based on a person's genuine experience but isn't ([CMA fake reviews guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews-cma208)), so an AI actor scripted as a satisfied customer carries legal risk on top of the trust problem.

## UGC vs influencer marketing

UGC buys content: a creator makes videos that your brand posts or runs as ads. Influencer marketing buys reach: the creator posts to their own followers. UGC is priced per video plus usage rights, while influencer fees rise with audience size, so the right choice depends on whether you need ads to test or attention from a particular community.

| | UGC | Influencer marketing |
|---|---|---|
| What you buy | Videos you license or own | A post to the creator's audience |
| Who posts it | Your brand, or the creator's handle as an ad you run | The creator, on their own account |
| What sets the price | Usage rights, hooks, edits, experience | Audience size, engagement, format |
| Best for | Paid ads, landing pages, testing | Awareness and trust within a community |

[Whito's 2026 UK figures](https://whito.co.uk/research/influencer-ugc-rates-uk/) show how fast influencer fees climb: about £20 to £150 per post for nano influencers (500 to 10,000 followers), £80 to £1,500 for micro (10,000 to 100,000) and £4,000 to £8,000 or more for macro creators (100,000 to 500,000). Whito says these are converted from largely US-dollar benchmarks and adjusted upward for UK audiences ([Whito](https://whito.co.uk/research/influencer-ugc-rates-uk/)).

In the UK, influencer posts that are ads must be obviously identifiable as ads ([CAP Code rule 2.1](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/02.html)).
